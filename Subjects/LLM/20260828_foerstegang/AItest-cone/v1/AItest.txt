This is an interesting fluid dynamics problem involving how water flows out of cone-shaped containers with differently oriented cones but otherwise identical conditions. Here's how we analyze it:

Both containers are filled with the same volume of water and have the same shape and size, so the total amount of water to empty is the same.
Both holes are the same size and shape, which means the size of the outlet (and hence the potential flow rate for a given pressure) is the same.
The difference is the cone's orientation:
Container A: pointy end on top, hole at the flat bottom.
Container B: flat side on top, hole at the pointy bottom.
How the water drains depends mainly on the pressure at the hole and the water height above it (hydrostatic pressure), as well as the shape-dependent flow behavior inside the container.

Key points:

In Container A, the hole is at the flat bottom, so the water column above the hole is longest (full height of the cone).
In Container B, the water level begins at the flat top, and the hole is at the pointy bottom. The cross-sectional area near the hole is very small (the tip), so the height of water above the hole is still the whole height of the cone, but the shape influences how the pressure and flow develop.
Because of the cone shape, the water pressure at the hole depends on the vertical height of water above it, which is the same for both containers when full.

However, as the containers drain:

In Container A (pointy end on top, hole at the base), the water level drops from the narrow top downward, but the large cross-sectional area at the hole remains full for nearly the entire draining process.
In Container B (flat end on top, hole at the point), the water level drops downward toward the very narrow bottom tip, reducing pressure and flow rate more quickly as the water volume decreases.
Intuition and simple fluid dynamics suggest that Container A will empty faster because the wider base allows a higher average flow rate as the water level falls, compared to Container B where the flow rate slows significantly as the water approaches the narrow tip.

So the true statement is:

Container A will empty before Container B.

reply


Worked for 1s
B will empty before A.
Both cones have the same volume, height $H$, base radius, and hole area. Outflow speed follows Torricelli’s law: $v = \sqrt{2gh}$ (same instantaneous head $h$ gives the same exit speed). What differs is how much volume sits at each height, so the time to drop the surface by $dh$ is proportional to the free-surface area $S(h)/\sqrt{h}$.

A (apex up, hole in the large base): $S(h)$ is large near the bottom. After the narrow top empties quickly you are left with a wide, shallow pool under very little pressure. That last large volume takes a long time.
B (apex down, hole at the tip): $S(h) \propto h^2$. Most of the water starts high (wide top) and therefore leaves under high head. As the level falls the remaining volume shrinks rapidly, so the surface drops faster and faster.

Integrating the resulting ODE gives emptying times in the exact ratio
$$T_B = \frac{3}{8}T_A.$$
(The same conclusion follows from the rearrangement inequality: pairing the large cross-sections with large $\sqrt{h}$ minimizes total time.)
Ideal-fluid theory and CFD both confirm the tip-down orientation finishes first. In a real container the large flat bottom of A can leave a thin residual film that never reaches a central hole, which only widens the gap.35 sources
