reply:


B will empty before A.

The key is that the water pressure at the hole depends only on the height of water above the hole, but the amount of water that has to drain at each height depends on the container's cross-sectional area.

Let the cone have height \(H\).

Container B — pointy end at the bottom

As the water drains, the water level gets lower, and the container gets narrower. So near the end, there is very little water spread over a small cross-sectional area.

That means the water level falls relatively quickly.

Container A — flat end at the bottom

As the water drains, the water level gets lower, but the container becomes wider. So near the end, there is a large amount of water for each small decrease in water level.

Thus the water level falls more slowly near the end.

Using Torricelli's law,

$$ Q = C A_h\sqrt{2gh} $$

and integrating over the changing cross-sectional area gives:

$$ t_A = \frac{16}{15}\frac{H^{5/2}}{C A_h\sqrt{2g}} $$

while

$$ t_B = \frac{2}{5}\frac{H^{5/2}}{C A_h\sqrt{2g}}. $$

Therefore,

$$ \frac{t_A}{t_B}=\frac{8}{3}\approx2.67. $$
So:

🥇 B empties first.

In fact, under the idealized assumptions (same hole, negligible viscosity, no air-flow complications), A takes about 2.67 times as long to empty as B.

The unintuitive part is that both containers have exactly the same volume—but the distribution of that volume above the hole is different.
